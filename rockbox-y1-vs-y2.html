Rockbox-Y1 · Rockbox-Y2

Rockbox-Y1 and Rockbox-Y2 are two different builds

They share a name, a look and a purpose, and almost nothing else. If you only remember one thing from this page: the model on the player decides the package, and the package names are different enough to check.

Why the model matters more than the name

Both ports replace the player software, both use the same MediaTek flashing route, and both look like Rockbox when they boot. The difference is the ROM: partition layout and images are built for one player. Flashing the Y1 package on a Y2 is not an experiment that teaches you something — it is the expensive mistake.

Updater CE prevents most of it by filtering releases by the model you select. When you are downloading by hand, the file name is the check: rom_y2.zip and rom_y2-*.zip are the Y2 packages, everything else in this comparison is Y1.

Choosing between them

  • You own a Y1 — Rockbox-Y1. Pick Type A unless you know the player needs Type B, then choose the 240p or 360p theme path.
  • You own a Y2 — Rockbox-Y2. Take a build listed for Y2 and read its release notes; the line is beta.
  • You own both — two separate installs. Nothing is shared except the fact that the app is the easiest way to flash either.

What is the same

The reason to install either is the same: a tag-based library, gapless playback, an equalizer, themes, and an offline player that does not ask for an account. The Why Rockbox page covers that side; this page is only about not mixing the packages up.
